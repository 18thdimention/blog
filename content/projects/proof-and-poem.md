## Jan 2024 ~ Aug 2024

A chapbook pairing poems with mathematical proofs that explore the same idea.

### Motivation

A proof and a poem are both attempts to say something true in as few words as possible. I wanted to test how far that analogy goes — whether the same concept, rendered in both languages, would illuminate or contradict itself.

### Structure

The chapbook has twelve pairs. Each opens with a statement — "There are more real numbers than natural numbers," or "Every continuous function on a closed interval attains its maximum" — followed by the proof on the left page and the poem on the right.

The proofs are rigorous but written for a general audience, with annotations. The poems are free verse, constrained only by the rule that they must address the same mathematical object.

### What I learned

The biggest surprise was how often the poem arrived at the proof's conclusion by a completely different path. The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem, for instance, is about the inevitability of accumulation — which is also what memory does.
